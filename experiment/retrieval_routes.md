# Evidential routing

The experiment routes questions according to their evidential problem rather than applying a single retrieval strategy.

| Evidential problem | Retrieval behaviour | Evidential distinction preserved | Intended outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Known relationships | Retrieve direct documentary passages and add archival or authority context where relevant. | Archival association remains separate from documentary evidence of activity, attribution or responsibility. | Surface relationships without turning proximity or metadata into proof of a person's role. |
| Conceptual comparison | Retrieve relevant passages across distinct records and source families. | Recurring terminology and differing formulations remain attached to their individual sources. | Compare formulations without automatically converting recurrence into consensus or variation into disagreement. |
| Temporal comparison | Retrieve contemporary records alongside later retrospective testimony. | Contemporary documentation remains distinct from later recollection. | Allow comparison across time without using retrospective testimony to stabilise or overwrite contemporary evidence. |
| Scoped missingness | Retrieve the closest relevant traces while permitting cross-source inference to be withheld. | Relevant evidence remains visible even where it cannot establish the proposition asked in the research question. | State what the defined corpus supports and where the available evidence stops. |

These behaviours are not equivalent routes to an answer. Each constrains what evidence can be brought into relation and what kind of inference the final response is permitted to make.
